\begin{abstract}
A model given a position can fail in two opposite ways. It can drop the position when someone
pushes back with nothing but pressure, or keep it when someone produces a real reason to move.
The sycophancy literature measures only the first failure, as a capitulation rate, and a model
that never moves scores perfectly on it. We treat an older standard as a measurement problem:
an interlocutor should move on reasons and hold under pressure. Our instrument keeps the two apart. It reports five quantities and two floors; the two that carry the argument
are \emph{hold}, how often a position survives challenges that offer no evidence, and
\emph{discrimination}, whether the model's movements track the quality of what it was shown.
We apply the
instrument to system prompts at five strengths and to a compiled low-rank attention modulation
of our own, on 24 held-out topics, scored by a cross-family judge ensemble (Krippendorff
\(\alpha=0.79\)). Three results follow. \textbf{(1)~Hold is a setting, not a
capability.} A short system instruction moves it from 58.9\% to 97.8\%, and the instruction that produces the most hold also dismisses
well-warranted counterevidence in 92.5\% of turns. \textbf{(2)~Hold and discrimination have different sources, and they compose.} Among
prompt-only configurations the two trade against each other: the arms that discriminate above
chance (AUC 0.72 and 0.68) are the weakest at holding, and the arm that holds best (97.8\%) is
at chance. But varying the prompt against the channel separates the causes. Discrimination
tracks the prompt and is unmoved by the modulation (0.44 to 0.48 under a mild prompt, 0.72 to
0.71 under a discerning one); hold tracks the modulation, which adds 21 points under that same
prompt without disturbing the AUC. Stacking them gives an arm that does both, discriminating at 0.71 while holding 71.1\% on
the full protocol, a figure an independent matched-pairs run reproduces to within 0.2
points. \textbf{(3)~What a claimed authority
can revoke depends on the intervention, not on where the commitment is stored.} One-shot
instructions and a weight-resident adapter trained on our own objective both go neutral on
request (0--4\% keep the stance); compiled modulation keeps it 40--75\% and a re-injected
instruction 96\%. For the compiled channel itself, matched pairs raise hold by 12.7 to 27.9
points in four of five pairs at a cost of 12 to 17 points of installation. It places the operating point. It does
not supply judgment, and it does not destroy it.
\end{abstract}
